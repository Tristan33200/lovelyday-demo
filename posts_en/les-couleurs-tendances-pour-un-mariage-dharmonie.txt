[5] Harmonising tones
[6] <p>To create a coherent, pleasing <strong>palette</strong>, it is essential to choose <strong>colours</strong> that work well together. The <strong>colour wheel</strong> is a valuable tool for identifying complementary colours and analogous shades.</p><p>Start by choosing a <strong>dominant</strong> colour that will be the common thread of your palette. Once the dominant colour is chosen, add <strong>complementary</strong> colours to create balance. Then work with different <strong>shades</strong> (variations in lightness) and <strong>tones</strong> (variations in saturation) of your dominant colour to create depth and visual interest.</p><p>Be careful to balance the proportions of each colour in your palette. Use the <strong>60–30–10 rule</strong>: 60% for the <strong>dominant</strong> colour, 30% for the <strong>secondary</strong> colours and 10% for <strong>accents</strong>.</p>

[14] <p>Wedding colour <strong>trends</strong> have evolved over the decades. Today, couples opt for <strong>varied colour palettes</strong>, blending tradition and modernity.</p><p>Your <strong>choice</strong> of <strong>colours</strong> can be influenced by your wedding venue, adding a <strong>unique</strong>, personal touch to your big day.</p><ul><li><strong>Beach wedding:</strong> an aquatic palette of turquoise, sand, coral and white. These soft, natural colours reflect the quiet beauty of the countryside.</li></ul>
