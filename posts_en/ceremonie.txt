[1] The civil ceremony
[2] <p>The <strong>civil wedding</strong>, held at the town hall, is the <strong>only official celebration</strong> that formally unites you in the eyes of the French state. It takes place in the presence of the <strong>mayor</strong> or a <strong>registrar</strong> of your town, the only people authorised to marry you officially. Without going to the town hall and hearing the <strong>reading of the articles of law</strong> on marriage, you are not legally married.</p><p>Town hall ceremonies are often <strong>short</strong>, leaving <strong>little room for festivities</strong>. That's why you might add a religious or symbolic ceremony on top of the civil one.</p>
[a3] Wedding planning
[4] The religious ceremony
[5] <p>A <strong>religious wedding</strong> is for couples of faith. Note that it has <strong>no legal value in the eyes of the law</strong>, only within your beliefs. Choose your place of worship according to your religion; the ceremony very often requires a period of religious preparation. On the big day you'll follow <strong>rituals</strong> and a whole <strong>protocol</strong> preparing you for married life. More personal and longer than the civil ceremony, it is always more moving.</p>
[7] The symbolic ceremony
[a8] ceremony
[9] <p>Although it has no legal value, this form of ceremony suits you if you don't want a religious wedding or simply want to <strong>say "yes" in a personal</strong>, <strong>free</strong> way. Designed around the couple, it is shaped by <strong>readings</strong>, <strong>speeches</strong> and <strong>rituals</strong> you choose in advance. It can be led by anyone close to you, so make the most of it! Or choose a professional <strong>officiant</strong>, who will tell your story and set the tone of the ceremony!</p>
