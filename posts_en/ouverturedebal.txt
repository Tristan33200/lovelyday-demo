[a1] first dance
[2] <p>The first dance is much more than a simple dance at your wedding. It is <strong>the first moment you appear as a married couple</strong>, a moment all your guests look forward to and one that will stay etched in your memories forever. It is the perfect opportunity to convey your emotions, show your closeness and create a unique memory.</p><p>But this moment can sometimes cause stress. Many couples feel uncomfortable on the dance floor, worry about looking awkward or don't know how to create a <strong>wedding first dance</strong> that truly reflects their personality. That is where the magic happens when you choose a <strong>personalised first dance</strong>, a <strong>choreography that reflects you</strong>, tells your story and shows you at your best.</p><p>In this article, we explore every step of creating a <strong>first dance that reflects you</strong>: from choosing the music to creating the choreography, through rehearsals, tips for managing stress and original ideas to surprise your guests.</p>
[3] Why personalise your first dance?
[a4] 2_YPS_2242_yoshipowershot.com
[5] <p>Every couple is unique, and your first dance should be too. The first dance isn't just a formality: it reflects your story and your closeness. Some brides dream of a classic waltz, others of a lively routine to a modern hit. Personalisation lets you:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Tell your story</strong>: your choice of music, your dance steps and the looks you exchange can illustrate your journey and your bond.</p></li><li><p><strong>Create a "wow" effect</strong>: your guests will long remember an original, authentic first dance.</p></li><li><p><strong>Feel at ease</strong>: adapting the dance to your level and style lets you fully enjoy this unique moment.</p></li></ul><p>For example, one couple I worked with chose to start their dance with a gentle ballad before switching to a more upbeat rhythm, adding a few humorous moves. The result: a moving, funny moment that was completely them.</p>
[6] Choosing the perfect music
[7] <p>It all starts with the music. The choice of song is crucial, because it will <strong>set the rhythm and atmosphere</strong> of your first dance.</p><h3>Types of music:</h3><ul><li><p><strong>Romantic and gentle</strong>: ideal for an intimate, moving moment. Slow songs let you express your emotions through every gesture.</p></li><li><p><strong>Upbeat and catchy</strong>: surprises your guests and makes the dance more festive. Perfect if you want an original first dance.</p></li><li><p><strong>Mix or mash-up</strong>: combines several songs to tell a story. For example, starting with a gentle song and then switching to an upbeat track for a surprise finale.</p></li></ul><p>A little tip: the average first dance lasts <strong>3 to 4 minutes</strong>. If you choose several songs, make sure the transitions are smooth and the dance stays coherent.</p><p>If you are short of inspiration for your first dance, here are nine songs that always work: <em>Perfect</em> by Ed Sheeran, <em>All of Me</em> by John Legend, <em>Thinking Out Loud</em>, <em>A Thousand Years</em>, <em>La Vie en Rose</em>, <em>Can't Help Falling in Love</em>, <em>You Are the Reason</em>, <em>Uptown Funk</em> and <em>Happy</em> by Pharrell Williams. You can use them as they are or create a medley for a <strong>personalised first dance</strong> that reflects you.</p>

[14] Creating a choreography that reflects you
[15] <p>Once you have chosen the music, it is time to think about the choreography. There's no need to be a professional dancer: what matters is feeling <strong>comfortable and natural</strong>.</p><h3>Steps to create your dance:</h3><ol><li><p><strong>Start simple</strong>: a few basic steps are enough to set the rhythm. A first dance doesn't need to be complicated to be effective.</p></li><li><p><strong>Add your personal touch</strong>: an improvised spin, a knowing look, a gesture that is truly you. This is what will make your dance unique.</p></li><li><p><strong>Rehearse regularly</strong>: practise several times before the wedding, even 10–15 minutes a day, to gain fluidity.</p></li><li><p><strong>Plan the transitions</strong>: if you use several songs or musical styles, work out how to move from one rhythm to another without a break.</p></li></ol><p>💡 Tip: you can call on a <strong>wedding dance coach</strong>. They will guide you in creating a professional yet natural choreography suited to your level and style.</p>
[16] Ideas to surprise your guests
[17] <p>A first dance that reflects you can include little surprises that will make your guests smile or move them:</p><ul><li><p>Start with <strong>a solo each, in turn</strong>, then come together for the finale.</p></li><li><p>Add a <strong>humorous</strong> or unexpected moment.</p></li><li><p>Use <strong>props</strong> such as a bouquet, ribbons or hats for visual effect.</p></li><li><p>Surprise everyone with a <strong>change of musical style</strong> or an unexpected dance sequence.</p></li></ul><p>The key is to stay <strong>authentic</strong>. If you attempt a sequence that is too complicated or doesn't suit you, the result can look forced. The best moments are those where you are simply yourselves.</p>
[18] Preparing the dance and managing stress
[19] <p>The big day can be stressful, but with good preparation you can fully enjoy this moment:</p><ul><li><p><strong>Rehearse in advance</strong>: practise for several weeks before the wedding so you are comfortable with the steps.</p></li><li><p><strong>Test the dance floor</strong>: make sure the space and the floor let you dance comfortably.</p></li><li><p><strong>Plan suitable shoes</strong>: comfortable and suited to your choreography.</p></li><li><p><strong>Practise in your outfits</strong>: do a few rehearsals in your wedding shoes and clothes to avoid unpleasant surprises.</p></li><li><p><strong>Breathe and have fun</strong>: even if you make a mistake, your guests will feel your authenticity and closeness.</p></li></ul><p>💡 Tip: some couples choose to do their final rehearsal with a videographer to see what works on screen and adjust the choreography before the wedding.</p>
[22] Anecdotes and inspiration
[23] <ul><li><p>One couple started their first dance with a romantic ballad, then switched to a Latin rhythm. Their guests were surprised and moved at the same time.</p></li><li><p>Another couple included symbolic gestures: a nod to their first meeting, a step they had been doing in their living room for years. The result: a deeply personal dance that told their story.</p></li><li><p>At some bohemian weddings, couples choose to end the dance with an improvised spin and a very upbeat finale to launch the party, surprising everyone and setting off applause.</p></li></ul><p>These examples show that a first dance that reflects you can be <strong>moving, funny, original or spectacular</strong>, depending on what you want to convey.</p>
[24] Tips for a successful first dance
[25] <ol><li><p><strong>Be yourselves</strong>: there's no point copying a viral video if it doesn't reflect you.</p></li><li><p><strong>Start early</strong>: plan your first dance several months before the wedding.</p></li><li><p><strong>Make videos</strong>: filming your rehearsals helps you fix small details and gain confidence.</p></li><li><p><strong>Choose the right music</strong>: it should move you and make you want to dance.</p></li><li><p><strong>Don't be afraid to ask for help</strong>: a coach or an experienced friend can give valuable advice.</p></li></ol>

Between the choice of music, a personalised choreography and little details that are truly you, every decision helps create an <strong>unforgettable, authentic</strong> moment.</p><p>Don't aim for perfection, but for sincerity. Use this moment to express your love, your closeness and your personality. Your guests will feel your authenticity and the magic will happen naturally.</p><p>Your first dance isn't just a dance; it is a <strong>moment that tells your story</strong> and will stay etched in your memories forever.
