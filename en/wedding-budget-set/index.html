Wedding budget: how do you set it?

Planning a wedding is as exciting as it is moving, but it can quickly become a source of stress if the budget isn't clearly defined from the start. Between your wishes, your Pinterest inspiration, advice from loved ones and suppliers' realities, it is easy to get carried away and lose control of spending. Yet setting a precise wedding budget is one of the most important steps for planning with peace of mind, whether you are marrying in Bordeaux, among the vineyards of Nouvelle-Aquitaine or elsewhere in the region.

Setting your budget at the very beginning gives you a clear framework, helps you make the right choices and, above all, lets you prioritise what really matters to you: an exceptional venue, a high-end caterer, elegant decor or a memorable atmosphere. It is also the best way to avoid nasty financial surprises a few weeks before the big day, when payments speed up and every detail counts.

A well-built budget helps you keep a realistic view, spread your spending wisely and plan your wedding with more serenity. Here is a step-by-step guide to help you set a realistic, coherent budget perfectly suited to your wishes.

Assess your financial resources

Before even thinking about flowers, the venue or the dress, it is essential to know how much you can really invest in your wedding. This step is often overlooked, yet it is the foundation of calm, coherent planning. A budget defined from the outset helps avoid frustration, difficult last-minute choices and, above all, unpleasant surprises.

Start by taking stock of your savings and current resources to determine what you can commit without putting your personal finances at risk. The aim is not to spend everything, but to set a realistic amount in line with your situation and priorities.

Next, clarify any contributions from your families or loved ones right from the start. Some help may be offered spontaneously, but it is important to discuss it openly to avoid any misunderstanding or unintended pressure. Knowing who is paying for what will help you build a more precise budget and make fairer choices.

Finally, always allow a safety margin of around 10 to 15%. The unexpected is common at weddings: an extra supplier, unpredictable weather that requires a fallback option, transport costs or small last-minute purchases. This reserve will help you stay calm and fully enjoy your preparations without unnecessary stress.

Define your priorities

Every couple has their own wishes and their own vision of the ideal wedding. For some, the dress or the photographer is non-negotiable, while for others it is above all the venue, the atmosphere or the meal that must be exceptional. There are no right or wrong priorities: what matters is that your budget reflects what really counts for you.

Take the time to list all the important categories (catering, decor, music, outfits, flowers, ceremony…) and rank your wishes in order of importance. This will help you spread your budget more wisely, investing more in what will make the difference to your day and your memories.

It is also essential to accept a degree of flexibility. Some secondary items can be adjusted without affecting the overall experience: slightly scaling back the flowers, choosing a simpler cake or limiting certain extras. This will help you stay within budget while keeping what matters most: a wedding that reflects you, coherent and free of financial stress.

Split the budget by category

To better anticipate your spending and keep a clear view of your planning, it is highly recommended to split your budget into different categories. This tells you where your money is going, helps you avoid oversights and, above all, lets you keep better control of your choices as you make bookings, whether in Bordeaux, in the vineyards of Nouvelle-Aquitaine or elsewhere in the region.

In general, the venue is often the largest share of the budget, as it sometimes includes extras such as furniture, accommodation or the hire of specific spaces. Next comes the caterer, along with drinks, often one of the biggest items, especially as the guest count grows.

The couple's outfits (dress, suit, accessories), as well as the photographer and videographer, are also significant expenses. They are essential because they shape both the experience of the day and the memories you will keep for life. So it is important to set aside an amount that matches the quality of service you want.

Decor, flowers and music (DJ or live band) must also be well planned, as they create the atmosphere and visual identity of your wedding. These are often areas where it is easy to overspend if you don't set limits from the start.

Finally, don't forget to set aside money for stationery, invitations, guest favours and a margin for the unexpected. Even with perfect organisation, there are always small last-minute expenses.

This breakdown isn't set in stone, of course: it should be adjusted to your priorities, your wedding style and the size of your event. The goal is to build a realistic, balanced budget that truly fits your vision of the big day.

Estimate the costs

Once the broad lines of your budget are defined, it is time for an essential step: finding out the real market prices. This is often when you realise that some services cost more (or sometimes less) than you imagined. This stage helps you build a concrete budget and avoid nasty surprises.

Start by comparing the prices of venues and caterers, as these are usually the biggest items. Take the time to request several quotes and check exactly what is included: furniture, staff, drinks, corkage, extra hours, and so on.

Next, look at the portfolios and quotes of suppliers such as photographers, florists, musicians or DJs to better understand how prices vary with experience, style and length of service. This will help you choose according to your expectations without exceeding your budget.

Also think about the often-forgotten extra costs: taxes, service charges, equipment hire, transport, accommodation and any tips. These small amounts can quickly add up and push up the final bill, especially if your wedding takes place in Bordeaux or elsewhere in Nouvelle-Aquitaine.

This step is essential to check whether your initial budget is truly realistic or needs adjusting. It also helps you make more strategic choices and stay in control throughout the planning.

Track and adjust the budget

To stay in control and organised throughout the preparations, it is essential to track your budget regularly. The simplest way is to use an Excel or Google Sheets spreadsheet, or a dedicated wedding app, to record every expense in real time: deposits paid, balances due, options added, extra costs… This gives you a clear, precise view of your budget at every stage.

This tracking is essential to avoid overspending, especially when several suppliers are booked over several months. By quickly seeing where you stand, you can adjust your decisions before it is too late and make more strategic choices.

It is also important to remember that a budget is never completely fixed. Quotes can change, you may fall in love with something (and push up the bill), or unexpected costs may arise: weather, extra guests, transport, overtime and so on. That is why it is essential to build some flexibility into your planning.

Finally, take time to review things regularly with your partner. Reassessing certain items together, comparing your priorities and adjusting some expenses if needed will help you stay within the overall limit while building a wedding that truly reflects you.

Save money without sacrificing your dreams

Good news: it is entirely possible to organise a magnificent wedding without blowing your budget. The key is to make the right choices at the right time, while keeping your priorities in mind. Saving doesn't mean giving things up, but rather optimising certain items so you can invest more in what really matters to you.

First, choosing a date outside the peak season (May to September) can significantly reduce costs. An autumn or winter wedding, or even a weekday one, often lets you benefit from better rates on venues and on some of the most in-demand suppliers in Bordeaux and Nouvelle-Aquitaine.

Next, choosing a venue that offers a complete package (with catering, furniture or decor included) can be an excellent way to save. These bundled services often reduce extra hire costs and also simplify the organisation.

Choosing local suppliers is also a smart strategy. As well as supporting professionals in your region, it limits travel, accommodation and equipment transport costs, which can quickly push up the final bill.

Finally, some personal touches can add a lot of charm while reducing costs. The invitations, part of the decor or even the guest favours can be made yourself, provided you allow enough time. These homemade details bring real authenticity and make your wedding even more unique.

With a little ingenuity and flexibility, you can create an elegant, memorable wedding that reflects you, while respecting your budget.

In short…

Setting your wedding budget is an essential step in turning your dreams into reality while keeping peace of mind and control over your finances. By assessing your resources, prioritising your wishes, splitting your spending by category and tracking your expenses carefully, you can plan a memorable wedding that reflects you, without nasty surprises, whether you celebrate in Bordeaux or elsewhere in Nouvelle-Aquitaine.

Good preparation, combined with a clear view of your priorities and a small margin for the unexpected, is the key to a perfectly successful big day, where you can enjoy every moment without stress.
